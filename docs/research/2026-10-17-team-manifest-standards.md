# Team manifests, agent permissions and budgets across the ecosystem × Ploeg — fit dossier

> Surveyed 2026-10-17 via four parallel research agents (published agent-description specifications; team constructs in multi-agent frameworks, Kubernetes CRDs and products; permission, capability and budget declaration formats of coding agents and gateways; a read-only seam map of this repository), with the schema URLs, registries and release pages re-checked by hand where a claim carries the verdict. This file is the full evidence trail. It records findings and proposed changes; it changes no decision by itself. The owner's question (2026-10-17): Ploeg should recognise *team building*: a Team with its own goal, permissions and capabilities as well as its models, harnesses and skills. Before Ploeg designs that manifest, is there a standard to reuse?

**Verdict: no published specification declares a team of agents with a goal, permissions, capabilities and a budget; the pieces exist apart, and Ploeg should compose them under its own Team manifest rather than adopt any team schema.** Of the twelve specifications and frameworks that describe an agent declaratively, only two consumer-less schemas (agents.yaml, OpenEnvelope) and one CRD (Ark) declare a team at all, and none of them, nor A2A 1.0, OASF 1.1, Oracle's Agent Spec, Microsoft's or Google's agent manifests, carries a monetary budget or a permission grant on an agent or a team. What has converged is smaller and worth taking: the `allow` / `ask` / `deny` effect on an action-and-resource rule (OpenCode 1 and 2, Claude Code, Cursor hooks, the Agent Skills `allowed-tools` field, Managed Agents' `always_allow` / `always_ask` / `auto`), budgets as an amount with a scope and a period (Anthropic spend limits and Managed Agents, LiteLLM team budgets), Agent Skills for the skill unit (already Ploeg's, ADR-0035 and ADR-0092), the A2A Agent Card as the *outward* description of what a Team offers (already watchlisted, ADR-0007), and a capability list that doubles as a scoped grant (Microsoft 365's declarative agent `capabilities`). The one product that models a team the way the owner means it, with budgets per agent and project, permission keys, a trust level and a team charter in Markdown, is Paperclip, which [ADR-0009](../adrs/0009-paperclip-mine-for-design-never-integrate.md) already says to mine for design and never depend on; its Teams Catalog and Agent fields are the best field list to mine. Ploeg's own prior decisions already point the same way: backlog #39 planned a Team manifest schema in YAML in 2026-07, and the roster verdict of 2026-07-28 chose a bespoke git-owned manifest over Backstage's descriptor.

## 1. What the question is

A Team in Ploeg is "a declarative manifest — name, Roles, harness image and model per Role, run strategy, resource/token budget, concurrency cap — that is the unit of claiming" (`docs/domain/model.yaml:287`). The manifest is real but split over two files and several ADRs (§L), has no goal or charter, no permissions object, and no single place that says what work the Team takes. The owner wants a Team that is built: a crew with a purpose, what it may do, what it can take on, and how it runs. The question for this sweep is which parts of that an existing standard already defines well enough to adopt, which parts to borrow a shape from, and which parts nobody has, so Ploeg writes them.

## 2. Layer map: which facet of a Team each standard claims

| Facet of a Team | Standards that declare it | Shape worth borrowing | Verdict for Ploeg |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goal or charter in words | every agent manifest has `description` or `instructions` (A2A, OASF, M365, Agent Framework, ADK, Agent Spec); no *team* schema has a typed goal; Paperclip keeps `COMPANY.md` and `TEAM.md`; CrewAI and MetaGPT put `goal` on each agent | a Markdown charter per Team, as Paperclip does, plus a one-line `description` the outward card reuses | Write it: `charter` on the Team manifest; nobody standardises it |

| Runtime binding: model, harness, image, sandbox | kagent `Harness {kagent|codex|claude|byo{image, command}}` and `SandboxTemplate`, OASF `locators {container_image, helm_chart}`, Agent Spec `llm_config`, Agent Framework `model.connection`; Ploeg has harness, image, sandbox and model per Role already | kagent's Harness CRD is the closest sibling and already in the September landscape | Keep Ploeg's; nothing to adopt |

| Capabilities and intake: what work it takes | nobody declares intake; A2A `skills[]` and OASF `skills` describe what an agent *offers* outward; M365 `capabilities` are grants | A2A `AgentSkill {id, name, description, tags, examples}` for the outward description of a Team | Write intake (boards, pull requests by author, follow-ups, admissions); describe outward as A2A skills when ADR-0007's facade comes |
| Skills | Agent Skills `SKILL.md` (~45 clients), OASF module `agentskills`, kagent `skills[] {name, source}`, agents.yaml `skills`; Ploeg has ADR-0035, ADR-0083 and the ADR-0092 catalogue | unchanged | Keep Agent Skills and the apm catalogue |
| The team object itself | agents.yaml `teams` (publisher unnamed, no consumer), OpenEnvelope Team Definition (6 stars, no consumer), Ark `TeamSpec` (CRD, members and strategy only), kagent (removed its Team CRD in v0.5.0), AutoGen `ComponentModel` (archived), Paperclip Teams Catalog (product) | Paperclip's field list as the design to mine; Ark's `members[{name, type: agent|team}]` as the only CRD shape | Ploeg's own manifest; adopt none |

Nobody claims the seams Ploeg's Team sits on: the Shift, the Lease, the Work Target that keeps a Team off any repository (R11), authorize-then-settle spend, and intake from trackers and forges. That is why no schema fits as a whole, and why the pieces above are shapes to borrow rather than dependencies to take.

## P. Permissions, capabilities and budgets: what exists (crawled 2026-10-17)

No cross-vendor standard declares what an agent, let alone a team, *may* do. What converges is a tri-state per action.

| Where | Shape | Granularity | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OpenCode 1.x `opencode.json` `permission` and `agent.<name>.permission` ([docs](https://opencode.ai/docs/permissions/)) | map action → `allow`/`ask`/`deny`; `bash` takes a pattern map, last match wins | per agent, per action (`read, edit, glob, grep, list, bash, task, skill, lsp, question, webfetch, websearch, external_directory, doom_loop`) | 1.19.3, 2026-10-16 |
| OpenCode 2.x `permissions` ([docs](https://opencode.ai/v2/docs/permissions/)) | ordered array `{action, resource, effect}`, wildcards in `resource`, last match wins, no match → `ask`; `agents.<id>.permissions` appended after global; separate `policies` array `{action: provider.use/tool.use/integration.use, resource, effect}` with no `ask` (hard deny) | per agent; actions renamed `shell`, `subagent` | 2.0.32, 2026-10-16; config layers incl. `/etc/opencode` and macOS managed plist |
| Claude Code `settings.json` `permissions.allow/ask/deny` ([docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions)) | rule strings `Tool(specifier)`: `Bash(git push *)`, `Edit(//secrets/**)`, `WebFetch(domain:…)`, `mcp__server__tool`; deny > ask > allow; `defaultMode`; `sandbox.network.allowedDomains`, `sandbox.filesystem.allowWrite/denyRead/denyWrite`; subagent frontmatter `tools`, `disallowedTools`, `permissionMode`, `model`, `maxTurns`, `skills`, `mcpServers`, `isolation: worktree`; hooks return `permissionDecision allow/deny/ask/defer` and can push `updatedPermissions` into a settings destination | per agent, per tool pattern | the richest in-tool vocabulary; single vendor |
| GitHub Copilot custom agents `.github/agents/*.agent.md` ([ref](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/reference/custom-agents-configuration)) | frontmatter `tools` (`execute|read|edit|search|agent|web|todo`, `server/tool`, `*`), `model`, `mcp-servers`; firewall allowlist is a repository UI setting, Bash only | per agent, tool aliases | no permission tri-state |
| Codex `config.toml` ([ref](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/config-file/config-reference)) | `approval_policy`, `sandbox_mode = read-only|workspace-write|danger-full-access`, `[permissions.<name>] filesystem.<path> = read|write|deny`, `network.domains.<pattern> = allow|deny`, `requirements.toml` with admin `allowed_sandbox_modes`/`allowed_permission_profiles`; Starlark command rules `prefix_rule(..., decision = allow|prompt|forbidden)`, strictest wins | named permission profiles | closest thing to a reusable *profile* object |
| Cursor hooks `.cursor/hooks.json` ([docs](https://cursor.com/docs/agent/hooks)) | hook stdout `{permission: allow|deny|ask}` per event (`beforeShellExecution`, `beforeMCPExecution`, …) | per event | rules `.mdc` have no permission fields |
| Anthropic Managed Agents (beta 2026-04-01, [docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/permission-policies)) | environment `networking {type: limited|unrestricted, allowed_hosts, allow_package_managers, allow_mcp_servers}`; per toolset `permission_policy {type: always_allow|always_ask|auto}`; session/deployment `budget {type: limit, max_list_cost {amount, currency}}`, `stop_reason: budget_reached` | per environment, per toolset, per session | the one hosted product that puts network, tool permission and money budget side by side |
| MCP 2026-07-28 ([tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/server/tools)) | `ToolAnnotations {readOnlyHint, destructiveHint, idempotentHint, openWorldHint}`; authorization = OAuth 2.1 with RFC 9728 and `resource`, scope step-up on 403, **no standard scope names, no per-tool permission mapping** | per tool, advisory ("clients MUST treat annotations as untrusted") | capability hints, not grants |
| A2A 1.0 AgentCard | `capabilities {streaming, push_notifications, extensions}`, `skills[] {id, name, description, tags, examples, security_requirements}`, `security_schemes`; no permission or budget field | per agent | describes what an agent offers, not what it may do |
| AWS AgentCore Policy (GA 2026-03, [docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/policy-conditions.html)) | Cedar: principals `AgentCore::OAuthUser` / `IamEntity`, actions per tool, `context.input.<arg>`, default deny, forbid wins | per gateway tool call | a policy language at the gateway, the shape ADR-0037/0078 already use with LiteLLM groups |
| Google ADK plugins | `before_tool_callback` etc.; no permission plugin shipped | code | |
| Agent Skills spec | experimental `allowed-tools: Bash(git:*) Read` in SKILL.md frontmatter, Claude Code rule syntax | per skill | the only cross-tool spec with a permission-shaped field |

Identity and authorization standards: the only WG-adopted IETF work is `draft-ietf-oauth-identity-assertion-authz-grant-04` (Cross-App Access, 2026-05-21); the agent-specific drafts (`draft-oauth-ai-agents-on-behalf-of-user-02`, `draft-liu-agent-operation-authorization-02`) are individual and expired. NIST CAISI's AI Agent Standards Initiative has an RFI and an NCCoE concept paper (2026), no draft standard. OWASP's Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 names Tool Misuse (ASI02), Identity and Privilege Abuse (ASI03) and Unexpected Code Execution (ASI05) as the risks a permission declaration addresses, and prescribes no format.

Budgets: no declaration standard. LiteLLM (`max_budget`, `budget_duration`, `tpm_limit`, `rpm_limit`, `model_max_budget`, `max_budget_in_team`, `soft_budget`), Anthropic spend limits (`amount` as a minor-unit string, `scope {organization|workspace|user}`, `period daily|weekly|monthly`) and Managed Agents budgets (`max_list_cost {amount, currency}`) are the three API-level shapes; OpenAI's limits are dashboard-only. The OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions (Development status, no release) carry token counts and `gen_ai.agent.id|name|version` but **no cost, price, currency or budget attribute**.

The domain model (`docs/domain/model.yaml:287`) defines a Team as "a declarative manifest — name, Roles, harness image and model per Role, run strategy, resource/token budget, concurrency cap — that is the unit of claiming", never naming a repository, forge or credential (R11), and lists `crew` under `avoid`. Its entity attributes `strategy` and `concurrency` have no config field; the code has Rounds and `maxRunning`. The operator API's Teams read serves `id`, `queueDepth`, `roles[]` (`id`, `queueDepth`, `harness`, `model`, `observedHarness`), `pinnedScopes[]`, `assignees[]`, and nothing of caps, pools, permissions or follow-up switches.

Prior decisions on the same ground: backlog #39 "Team manifest schema — YAML: name, Roles, strategy, budget; validated on apply, mirrored to the `teams` table" and #40 `ploegctl apply` (planning record, 2026-07-22); the roster verdict [2026-07-28-agent-roster-ssot](2026-07-28-agent-roster-ssot.md): one git-owned `org.yaml` in the deployment repository plus a reconciler, every other system a consumer, with the owner's decision that it is a bespoke minimal schema, not Backstage's descriptor; [ADR-0007](../adrs/0007-a2a-adopt-nothing-watchlist-a-facade.md) with backlog #102's "skills = teams" mapping for a future A2A facade; [ADR-0092](../adrs/0092-a-team-pins-its-skills-in-a-catalogue-that-ploegd-fetches-once-and-serves-by-digest.md) choosing the Agent Package Manager (`apm.yml`) format for a Team's skills; [2026-10-13-sharing-agent-skills](2026-10-13-sharing-agent-skills.md): "the single-skill format is standard, everything above it is per tool".

## R. Recommendations

1. **Keep the Team manifest Ploeg's own, in one place.** Gather what is split over the chart's `executor.teams[]` and the config file's `teams.<name>` into one declaration per Team with five parts: `charter` (goal in words, the default brief and the outward description), `crew` (today's plan and per-Role runtime, plus a `description` per Role), `may` (permissions), `intake` (what work the Team takes), `skills` (the ADR-0092 catalogue). Backlog #39 and #40 planned exactly this manifest in 2026-07; the roster verdict chose a bespoke schema over Backstage's. A proposed ADR records the shape.
2. **Borrow the permission record, not a grammar.** `may` is an ordered list of `{action, resource, effect}` with `effect ∈ allow | ask | deny`, OpenCode 2's shape, because it is YAML-native and every harness's native format can be rendered from it: Claude Code `permissions.allow/ask/deny` rules, OpenCode 1 maps and 2 arrays, Codex profiles, OpenHands' ACP permission mode. Ploeg's own actions sit beside harness actions: `write` (a branch and a push credential), `comment`, `createWork` (`proposed` or `queued`, today's `createdWork.autoDispatch`), `egress` (ADR-0037's allowlist), `tools` (ADR-0078's MCP groups). `ask` means a person decides through the existing `needs_human` path. The worker already keeps credentials the harness never sees (ADR-0034), so the manifest decides and the worker enforces.
3. **Borrow the budget shape.** A Team's money is `{amount, currency, period}` at three scopes Ploeg already has: a Shift pool, a Role cap per Run, and a monthly allowance (today only for Asks, ADR-0082). Amounts stay decimal strings, as the chart writes them; `period` follows Anthropic's `daily | weekly | monthly`. The gateway keeps enforcing (ADR-0008).
4. **Write intake; describe it outward as A2A skills.** Intake lists the boards whose assignment dispatches here (today's roster), the pull requests of listed authors on named target keys with an instruction (the route this branch adds under `targets`), the forge follow-ups, and whether operator admissions may name the Team. A target key, never a repository, keeps R11. When ADR-0007's facade comes, each Team's charter and intake become one `AgentSkill` on Ploeg's Agent Card, which is what backlog #102 already says ("skills = teams").
5. **Mine Paperclip's Teams Catalog and Agent fields for the field list** (`budgetMonthlyCents` at three scopes with warn-at-80 % and pause-at-100 %, `permissions`, `capabilities`, `trustLevel`, `requiredSkills`, `TEAM.md`, `IssueExecutionPolicy {stages, maxReviewRounds}`) under ADR-0009's standing rule: design, never a dependency.
6. **Adopt no team schema.** agents.yaml has no named publisher and no consumer; OpenEnvelope has six stars and no consumer; Ark's `TeamSpec` is members and a chat strategy on a Kubernetes API Ploeg does not run; kagent removed its own. A1 and A2 of the gotchas apply: pre-1.0, single or no vendor, no reference implementation in use.
7. **Re-confirm three standing records with this evidence**: ADR-0007 (A2A 1.0.1 still has no team, budget or permission field; the card remains the outward description only), ADR-0009 (Paperclip now ships the team object the owner describes; mine it, still never depend), ADR-0092 (OASF 1.1.0 carries an `agentskills` module beside its `agentspec` and `mcp` integrations, so the Agent Skills unit is the one both the catalogue and the registries agree on).

## T. Re-evaluation triggers

* A foundation-governed specification (A2A, OASF, the Agentic AI Foundation, NIST's agent initiative) adds a team object, a permission grant or a monetary budget to an agent description.
* agents.yaml or OpenEnvelope names a publisher and ships a consumer that reads the team schema.
* kagent or Ark re-adds or extends a Team CRD with a budget or permissions, or kagent is deployed in homelab-cluster.
* Agent Skills' `allowed-tools` leaves experimental, or the spec gains a per-skill permission or budget field.
* The OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions add a cost or budget attribute.
* anthropics/claude-code#89509 (team definitions with roles and budgets) ships.
* Paperclip publishes its Team or Agent schema as a standalone, versioned specification.

## U. Method and limits

Four agents ran in parallel on 2026-10-17; the specification agent's claims about field names were re-checked against the schema URLs for A2A (`a2a.proto`), agents.yaml and OpenEnvelope, the npm registry for OpenCode, and GitHub for Paperclip (`docs/api/agents.md`: `capabilities` is free text, `budgetMonthlyCents` and `chainOfCommand` present), Ark (`TeamSpec` fields) and kagent (`v1alpha3` has no Team kind).
