# Code of conduct

Ploeg follows the [CNCF Community Code of Conduct v1.3](https://github.com/cncf/foundation/blob/main/code-of-conduct.md). It applies in Ploeg's repository, issues, pull requests and Discussions, and anywhere someone's words or actions are directed at Ploeg or at its participants because of their part in it.

This page says how to report a possible violation, who handles it, and how to appeal. It follows CNCF's [incident resolution procedures](https://github.com/cncf/foundation/blob/main/code-of-conduct/coc-incident-resolution-procedures.md) and [jurisdiction policy](https://github.com/cncf/foundation/blob/main/code-of-conduct/coc-committee-jurisdiction-policy.md) wherever they can apply to a project that is not part of CNCF.

## Report an incident

If someone is in immediate physical danger, call your local emergency number.

You choose where to report. You do not have to try the maintainers first.

1. **To Ploeg's maintainers.** The [maintainers](MAINTAINERS.md) are Ploeg's code of conduct responders. Report privately through GitHub's private reporting form: on the repository's **Security** tab choose **Report a vulnerability** ([direct link](https://github.com/ploeg-hq/ploeg/security/advisories/new)), and start the title with `Conduct:`. The form is meant for security reports, but Ploeg's maintainers also take conduct reports there, and only the maintainers and the people they invite can read it. A maintainer answers within three business days and does not turn a conduct report into a published advisory. If the report is about a maintainer, use route 2.
2. **To the CNCF Code of Conduct Committee.** Write to [conduct@cncf.io](mailto:conduct@cncf.io), which reaches every primary member of the [committee](https://www.cncf.io/conduct/committee/), or write to one member directly. To report anonymously, send a direct message to one member on the CNCF Slack and say you want to report anonymously; that member shares the report with the committee without your identity. CNCF says to expect a response within three business days.
3. **To GitHub.** Content and behaviour on GitHub also fall under GitHub's own rules. Use **Report content**, then **Report abuse to GitHub Support**, on the issue, pull request, comment or Discussion, or GitHub's [abuse report form](https://support.github.com/contact/report-abuse?category=report-abuse&report=other&report_type=unspecified). GitHub Support handles these reports, not Ploeg's maintainers.

**Ploeg is not a CNCF project.** The committee's jurisdiction policy covers CNCF projects, so until Ploeg is accepted the committee may decline a Ploeg report. If it declines, the report comes back to the Ploeg maintainers who have no conflict in it. If there is no such maintainer, see the next section.

### What to include

As far as you can: who was involved, or a description or handle if you do not know names, including witnesses; what happened, when and where; which part of the code of conduct you think was broken; and any screenshots or links.

## When a maintainer is involved

A maintainer has a hard conflict, as CNCF's [conflict of interest rules](https://github.com/cncf/foundation/blob/main/code-of-conduct/coc-incident-resolution-procedures.md#conflicts-of-interest) define it, when they are the person reported or a person harmed; when they have a close personal or professional relationship with either; or when they have another personal interest in the outcome. A maintainer with a hard conflict takes no part in handling the report and sees none of its confidential details.

When most of Ploeg's responders have a hard conflict, the report goes to the CNCF Code of Conduct Committee. CNCF's jurisdiction policy asks the same of its own projects. While Ploeg has one maintainer, every report about that maintainer goes there.

If the committee declines a report about the sole maintainer, Ploeg has no responder without a conflict. The maintainer then does not judge their own case. They step back from the space the report is about, such as the thread or the Discussion category, and keep the report on file. When Ploeg next has a maintainer without a conflict, or joins CNCF, the report goes to them with the reporter's consent. Route 3 stays open throughout. Closing this gap is one reason Ploeg needs a [second maintainer](GOVERNANCE.md#continuity-and-succession).

## What responders do

1. Acknowledge the report within three business days.
2. Keep the reporter's identity and the details confidential. Share them only with the people who need them to resolve the report, and never with a responder who has a hard conflict.
3. Look at the evidence and talk to the people involved. Tell the person reported that there is a report about them, unless the report is clearly without merit or made in bad faith, or telling them would risk retaliation or lost evidence.
4. Act on interim risk while the report is open: hide or lock content, or block an account from the `ploeg-hq` organisation for the time being.
5. Choose a remedy that stops the harm and fits it. CNCF's examples are a warning, removing content, a public or private apology, a temporary or permanent ban, removal from a role, and coaching. CNCF frames remedies as repairing harm and protecting the community.
6. Tell the reporter the outcome, and the person reported if they were told of the report.
7. Keep a private record of each report: the date, who handled it, and the outcome.

Ploeg's maintainers do not name a person reported in public. Once Ploeg is a CNCF project, they consult Linux Foundation legal counsel before a ban longer than two months or naming a person reported, as the jurisdiction policy requires.

Retaliating against someone who reported in good faith, or who helped as a witness, is itself a violation. So is giving knowingly false information to the people handling a report.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this code of conduct may be removed from their role ([removal](GOVERNANCE.md#removal-for-cause)).

## Appeal

Anyone involved, whether the reporter, a person harmed or the person reported, may appeal a decision by Ploeg's maintainers to the CNCF Code of Conduct Committee at [conduct@cncf.io](mailto:conduct@cncf.io). Under CNCF's jurisdiction policy the committee can overturn a project decision when the project should have transferred the report, when someone with a hard conflict decided it, when the evaluation was unfair, when the project broke its own or CNCF's rules, or when the outcome is grossly out of proportion. Until Ploeg is a CNCF project the committee may decline the appeal. Then the appeal goes to a maintainer who took no part in the decision, if there is one.

## Once Ploeg is a CNCF project

- The maintainers tell the CNCF Code of Conduct Committee about every report they receive, with a summary and the name of the person reported, and tell the committee the outcome. CNCF uses this to spot repeated behaviour across projects.
- The maintainers transfer a report to the committee when it concerns more than one CNCF project, when most of them have a hard conflict, when laws may have been broken, or when it carries legal risk.
